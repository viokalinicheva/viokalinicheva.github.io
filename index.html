Beyond the Job Title

Mapping data science skills and career pathways

Which occupations are most similar to Data Scientists when we compare the work itself—not just the title? I built an O*NET career map to support more grounded exploration of transferable skills.

Data & method
882 occupations × 109 standardized Importance ratings from O*NET 31.0. I audited reliability flags, used PCA for exploration, ranked similarity in the full feature space, and tested alternative filtering, distance, and weighting choices.
Finding
Statisticians were the closest occupation. The first two principal components explained 46.3% of variation; 14 were needed to reach 80%, so the two-dimensional map did not determine the ranking.
Decision boundary
This is a map of occupations, not people. It does not predict hiring success or individual ability. A silhouette score of 0.209 supports only broad clustering—not sharply separated career families.

Employee Attrition Early Warning

A useful risk score needs an honest error story

Can workplace characteristics help identify attrition risk for constructive retention conversations? I compared models on fictional employee data, then made the false-alert tradeoff central to the recommendation.

Data & method
1,470 fictional IBM employee records; 1,176 training and 294 untouched test records. I removed constants and an identifier, kept preprocessing inside a pipeline, and selected Logistic Regression using training-only stratified cross-validation.
Finding
Test ROC-AUC was 0.802 and precision-recall AUC was 0.557. At the default 0.50 threshold, the model identified 30 of 47 departures, but produced 56 false alerts.
Decision boundary
63.8% recall and 34.9% precision are a tradeoff, not a deployment endorsement. Fictional data and predictive associations cannot establish causes. Any real use requires current organizational validation, fairness checks, and human oversight.

MovieLens Recommendation System

A better discovery baseline, without a dense ratings matrix

Can one liked movie lead to useful alternatives? I audited all 32 million ratings, built a sparse item-to-item model, and compared retrieval with global popularity.

Data & method
32,000,204 ratings; chunked validation, positive-interaction filtering, TF-IDF, and validation-selected 64-factor SVD. Every reserved target interaction is excluded from training.
Finding
Hit Rate@10 was 8.99% versus 3.16% for popularity on 9,769 supported test cases. Those cases cover 97.93% of reserved test users; coverage-adjusted retrieval is also reported.
Decision boundary
A transductive historical benchmark—not a global-time backtest or an online satisfaction result. Earlier user histories remain in training, and timestamp ties do not establish true sequence.

Code & documentation availablePython / pandas / visual storytelling

TSA Complaint Data Quality

Reporting gaps are part of the service story

What can service leaders learn from complaint counts without confusing missing attribution with airport performance? I reconciled nested data grains before comparing reporting patterns.

Data & method
1,021,281 complaints in Data Liberation Project–standardized TSA reports, January 2015–January 2024. All 41,721 airport-month totals reconcile across category and subcategory tables.
Finding
42.9% of complaints have no airport attribution, while 47.4% have imputed or ambiguous upstream subcategory labels. Those limitations stay visible instead of disappearing during cleaning.
Decision boundary
Submitted counts are not complaint rates, safety scores, or fair airport rankings. Passenger denominators are absent, and January 2024 is partial. Improve attribution before comparing service performance.

Childcare Affordability Across U.S. Counties

Compare the same places, in the same year's dollars

How did infant-care price-to-income ratios change in consistently observed counties? I repaired a nominal-versus-real currency mismatch and made missing coverage part of the story.

Data & method
34,567 county-year records from the Department of Labor's historical childcare database. A balanced panel of 1,273 counties uses fixed 2018 household weights and same-year price and income.
Finding
The weighted mean county ratio rose from 18.52% to 19.92% during 2008–2018, a 1.40-percentage-point increase. Missing infant prices—31.75% of source rows—were not imputed.
Decision boundary
These are historical county ratios, not current family spending or a national median. Household weights do not identify childcare users; source imputations and nonrandom missingness remain limitations.

Kia & Hyundai Vehicle Theft

A historical monitoring signal—not a deployed alert

Which reported increases survive consistent city and time coverage? I audited four sources separately rather than joining overlapping jurisdictions into a supposed national total.

Data & method
An R workflow checks city-month keys, missing reporting, and count-based percentages. Nine of thirteen cities have complete 2019–2022 monthly coverage and form the fixed comparison panel.
Finding
Reported Kia and Hyundai thefts in that panel rose from 2,315 in 2021 to 12,592 in 2022—443.9%. All nine cities increased. The audit also found 42 source percentages inconsistent with displayed counts.
Decision boundary
Counts do not measure per-vehicle risk or a national rate, and timing does not identify a social-media effect. A prospective alert needs exposure denominators, thresholds, and independent evaluation.

Retail Sales Forecasting During Structural Change

Twelve forecasts. Twelve underestimates.

Can a simple historical trend support planning through a rapid change? I compared three baselines using rolling validation, then kept the model's failure year in full view.

Data & method
354 monthly Census retail-trade observations, January 1992–June 2021. Five expanding-window folds select a linear trend before the final twelve months are scored.
Finding
RMSE increased from 11,343 in validation to 66,433 in the test year, in millions of nominal U.S. dollars. The model underestimated sales in all twelve test months; test error was 5.86 times validation error.
Decision boundary
A failure analysis, not a production forecast. July 2021 revision-vintage values make this a fixed-vintage retrospective backtest, not a real-time historical forecast. Sales are seasonally adjusted, not inflation-adjusted.

The Lottery Pattern Trap

Historical frequency is not a validated forecast

Do apparent “hot numbers” establish an advantage? I separated games and historical rules, corrected for sampling without replacement, and checked how source revisions affect the conclusion.

Data & method
17,472 supplied drawings from three New York Lottery snapshots. Main-number tests use a common October 2017–April 2024 window, game-specific pools, and Holm multiplicity adjustment.
Finding
The three snapshot checks do not reject uniform marginal selection: p = 0.460, 0.475, and 0.503. Later official Powerball additions change its p-value to 0.407 without changing that decision.
Decision boundary
No rejection is not proof of randomness or predictive ability. Source versions are preserved rather than silently mixed, and certified official records prevail. No winning-ticket or betting-return claim is made.

White House Visitor Operations

Appointment-access records are not attendance

What can historical public records tell an operations analyst without exposing individual visitors? I harmonized twenty-five official releases and published only aggregate views.

Data & method
1,207,458 source rows across 2021–2023. One-file-at-a-time processing checks schemas, dates, duplicate rows, and reconciliation; names and individual schedules stay out of public outputs.
Finding
Removing 1,202 exact within-release duplicates leaves 1,206,256 disclosed records. The dominant WH location code accounts for 81.3%; it is retained rather than hidden to emphasize smaller locations.
Decision boundary
Disclosed records are not unique people, realized visits, political influence, or staffing hours. Policy exclusions and partial 2021 coverage prevent an unqualified demand forecast.

Netflix Global Content Performance

Separate a peak from persistence—and both from profit

How do charting films and TV seasons differ in peak reach, repeated appearances, and country breadth? I kept the metrics separate instead of combining incompatible windows into one score.

Data & method
Official weekly Top 10 data, bounded to the historical April 2024 endpoint. Views comparisons use 44 common weeks and 741 distinct title-season units, preserving seasons and counting observed chart weeks once.
Finding
Non-English TV seasons averaged 2.97 observed Global Top 10 weeks versus 1.98 for English films. That describes persistence within a selected chart window—not how many subscribers stayed.
Decision boundary
Top 10 selection, release schedules, availability, and window censoring shape comparisons. Views are not unique viewers; chart popularity does not establish quality, retention, profitability, or a causal language effect.
